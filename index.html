Thy grading —
BTA 2023 ultrasound classification and management guidelines. U-category reflects ultrasound features only; final management must integrate clinical context, patient factors, and cytology (Thy grading) when available.
U1 Normal ~0%

Normal thyroid appearances with no discrete nodule identified, or an incidental nodule with no suspicious features on a high-quality study.

No FNA required. No follow-up required.

U2 Benign <1%

Features consistent with benign pathology: pure cystic nodule, spongiform / microcystic nodule, hyperechoic nodule with comet-tail artefacts, or a nodule with typical benign features (well-defined, isoechoic, no suspicious calcification).

No FNA required. Routine clinical follow-up only; USS follow-up not routinely indicated unless large (>35 mm) or clinical concern.

U3 Indeterminate / Equivocal ~5–10%

Features that are neither clearly benign nor clearly suspicious. Includes: predominantly solid isoechoic nodule with lobulated margin, mixed cystic-solid nodule with solid mural nodule, or subtle suspicious features insufficient for U4.

FNA if ≥ 20 mm (or smaller if rapid growth, symptomatic, or clinical concern).

If no FNA: USS follow-up at 3–6 months, then annually for 2 years if stable. Reclassify if features change.

U4 Suspicious ~60–75%

One or more of: solid hypoechoic nodule with irregular / lobulated margin, microcalcification within a solid or mixed component, taller-than-wide shape, disrupted peripheral calcification.

FNA recommended for all nodules ≥ 10 mm. For nodules <10 mm, clinical and MDT discussion advised before proceeding.

Cytology should be reported using the Thy grading system (Thy 1–5).

U5 Malignant >95%

Highly suspicious features present: markedly hypoechoic solid nodule, taller-than-wide shape, clearly irregular / spiculated margin, extra-thyroidal extension, or suspicious lymph nodes with loss of fatty hilum, microcalcification, or cystic change.

FNA and urgent MDT referral. If FNA confirms malignancy (Thy 5), proceed directly to surgical planning. Do not delay management for repeat sampling where features are overtly malignant.

Core needle biopsy may be appropriate for large fixed masses or where FNA is non-diagnostic.

Thy cytology grading: Thy 1 = non-diagnostic; Thy 2 = non-neoplastic; Thy 3a = atypia of undetermined significance; Thy 3f = follicular lesion / neoplasm; Thy 4 = suspicious for malignancy; Thy 5 = diagnostic of malignancy. FNA reports should always be correlated with the U-category. Based on BTA 2023 Guidelines.
🧱 Composition Choose 1
Pure cystic
Entirely fluid-filled with no solid component. May contain colloid or blood products. Almost always benign (U2).
Microcystic / spongiform
More than 50% of the nodule volume is composed of small cystic spaces (aggregated microcysts). The "sponge" appearance is highly specific for benign colloid nodule. Classified U2 regardless of size.
💡 The aggregated microcystic spaces often produce comet-tail reverberation artefacts, confirming colloid content.
Mixed cystic and solid
Contains both cystic and solid components. Classification depends on the features of the solid component. A solid mural nodule with suspicious characteristics upgrades the classification towards U4.
⚠️ Always evaluate the solid component independently. A purely smooth mural nodule without suspicious features suggests U2–3; irregular or calcified solid elements raise suspicion to U4.
Solid
No or negligible cystic component. Solid nodules must be assessed with echogenicity, shape, margin, and calcification to assign U-category.
Uncertain (heavily calcified)
When coarse calcification produces extensive posterior acoustic shadowing preventing assessment of internal composition. Treat as solid for classification purposes and assess accessible margins carefully.
🔊 Echogenicity Choose 1 · for solid component
Anechoic
No internal echoes. Applies only to pure cystic nodules. Classified U2.
Hyperechoic or isoechoic
Equal to or greater echogenicity than surrounding thyroid parenchyma. Generally favours benign aetiology in the context of no other suspicious features (U2–3). Hyperechoic nodules with peripheral halo and well-defined margin are characteristic of benign adenoma.
Hypoechoic
Less echogenic than normal thyroid parenchyma but more echogenic than adjacent neck musculature. A single moderately suspicious feature; U-category is determined by co-existing features.
Markedly hypoechoic
Less echogenic than the adjacent strap muscles. Highly suspicious feature; alone sufficient to consider U4–5, especially when solid.
⚠️ All echogenicity comparisons are made against adjacent thyroid parenchyma, not the lesion itself. Ensure you are comparing to normal-appearing parenchyma, not to thyroiditis-affected tissue.
📐 Shape Choose 1 · assess in transverse plane
Wider than tall
The transverse diameter exceeds the anteroposterior (AP) dimension in the axial plane. Favours benign morphology; most benign nodules expand horizontally along tissue planes.
Taller than wide
AP dimension exceeds transverse diameter in the axial plane. Reflects invasive growth perpendicular to tissue planes. An independent predictor of malignancy; alone sufficient to upgrade to U4 in a solid nodule.
💡 Measure in the axial (transverse) plane only. Shape assessment in the longitudinal plane is not used for U-classification. AP dimension = depth from skin surface to posterior nodule wall.
🔲 Margin Choose 1
Smooth / well-defined
A clearly demarcated, even margin without protrusions, notching, or spiculation. A halo sign (hypoechoic rim) is a specific marker of encapsulation and benign aetiology.
Ill-defined
Margin merges imperceptibly with adjacent parenchyma but without clear invasion. This is a benign feature in the BTA scheme — typically seen in thyroiditis. Do not conflate with irregular or spiculated margin.
Lobulated or irregular
Protrusions or angulations extending into adjacent thyroid tissue (lobulated), or a jagged/spiculated interface. Either pattern is a suspicious feature contributing to U4 classification.
Extra-thyroidal extension
Unequivocal invasion beyond the thyroid capsule into adjacent soft tissues (strap muscles, trachea, oesophagus, vessels). Highly suspicious; alone classifies as U5. Only score when clearly present — do not infer from abutment of the capsule alone.
⚠️ Extra-thyroidal extension should only be reported when clearly visible. Tangential imaging or anterior bulging is not sufficient. If uncertain, report as U4 with comment.
✨ Calcification Select most suspicious present
None / comet-tail artefact
No calcification identified, or V-shaped comet-tail reverberation artefacts (>1 mm). Comet-tail artefacts arise from colloid-filled microcysts and are a benign feature, not to be confused with microcalcification.
Macrocalcification
Coarse calcification producing posterior acoustic shadowing. May be central, peripheral, or in a "eggshell" pattern. Intermediate level of suspicion; classified within U3–4 context depending on co-existing features.
Peripheral / rim calcification
Complete or incomplete calcification at the nodule margin. When peripheral calcification is disrupted or has soft tissue break-through, this substantially raises the risk of malignancy (U4–5).
Microcalcification (punctate echogenic foci)
Tiny (<1 mm), non-shadowing, punctate echogenic foci within the solid component. Pathologically correspond to psammomatous calcifications, highly characteristic of papillary thyroid carcinoma. A strongly suspicious feature contributing to U4–5.
⚠️ Distinguish carefully from comet-tail artefact. True microcalcifications cast no posterior shadow and produce no V-shaped posterior artefact. Foci in cystic spaces with posterior artefact are colloid — not suspicious.
🩸 Vascularity Supplementary feature
Absent or peripheral only
No internal vascularity on colour or power Doppler. Peripheral (rim) vascularity alone is not suspicious and is commonly seen in adenomatous nodules.
Increased internal vascularity
Marked internal vascularity with chaotic or disorganised flow pattern. A supplementary suspicious feature that may support U4 classification when other features are borderline. Not used as an isolated criterion for classification.
💡 Vascularity assessment is Doppler-setting dependent. Ensure gain is optimised before documenting vascularity. Power Doppler is more sensitive than colour Doppler for low-flow states.
🔗 Lymph Nodes Cervical chain assessment
Normal
Oval morphology with preserved fatty hilum, hilar vascularity, and a hypoechoic cortex of uniform thickness (<3 mm). Normal nodes at level VI may be smaller and rounder. Reactive nodes may be enlarged but retain internal architecture.
Suspicious
One or more of: rounded shape (short:long axis ratio >0.5), loss of fatty hilum, microcalcification, cystic change, or peripheral / hyperechoic echogenicity. Suspicious nodes at any level of the central or lateral compartment in the context of a thyroid nodule strongly suggest malignancy and mandate U5 classification.
⚠️ Even a small (sub-centimetre) thyroid nodule should be classified U5 if any cervical lymph node shows unequivocal suspicious features. Node status takes priority over nodule size in management decisions.
